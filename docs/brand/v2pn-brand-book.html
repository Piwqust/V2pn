Chapter 01

Story

01

One way in.
Two ways out.

V2pn is a desktop client for people who want the internet to just work. Underneath it is v2rayN with Xray and sing-box. On top it is one screen: pick a place on the map and press Connect.

Every connection has two ways out. Local sites go direct, so banks and government services keep working. Everything else goes through a server the person chose. The logo draws that fork. The glass shows the second way: light gets through, the details stay behind the ribs.

Principle

One tap, then quiet.

The app asks for one decision, the place. Mode and route have good defaults. After Connect, it gets out of the way.

Principle

Show the route.

People should always see which way their traffic goes: direct or through a server, and which server.

The fork

A road seen from above.

  1. A
    Stem: youOne way in. The mark is anchored at the bottom, where the person is.
  2. B
    Left branch: directGoes straight up after one step aside. Local sites take this way.

    Right branch: through a serverTurns out at 45° toward the world, then stops square.
  4. D
    Square cutsBoth ways end somewhere real. No arrowheads, no rounding.

Construction

Built from one stroke.

The unit u is the stroke width. Everything else is a multiple of it, and every diagonal is 45°. Redraw the mark from these numbers, never by tracing.

Wordmark

Drawn, not typed.

The wordmark is Inter ExtraBold turned into outlines, tracked −3.5% with three pairs spaced by hand: v2 closer, 2p looser. Always use the artwork. Typing “v2pn” in a font gives a different word.

Chapter 05

Glass

05

Light gets through. The details stay yours.

Scene: people · 44 ribs

Ribbed glass is what offices and bathrooms use for privacy: you see that someone is there, never who. V2pn uses it for everything that goes through a server.

The rule is literal. Things that are private sit behind glass. Things that are direct, like the logo, text and buttons, stay sharp on top of it. Never put words behind the ribs.

Anatomy

Ribs36–64across the frame, always vertical, all the same width
Refraction1.6–2.0×each rib squeezes a slice that much wider than itself
Softness4–8%of frame height, how far the subject is blurred before the glass

Do

  • Keep ribs vertical and even, like a real pane.
  • Put one subject behind the glass and let it fill the frame.
  • Place the logo and text sharp, on top, in white.
  • Stay inside Signal, Flare and Tunnel.

Don't

  • Horizontal, curved or uneven ribs.
  • Rainbow edges, chrome or chromatic split.
  • Text, UI or the logo behind the glass.
  • Glass on anything a person has to read or tap.

Chapter 06

Map

06

The map is the app's home screen, so it is also the brand's landscape. It is drawn from the same data as the app: Natural Earth countries in Mercator, 2400 × 1560.

Land is a grid of graphite dots. Servers are white dots; the active one is Signal with a ring. Labels are mono, set next to the dot, never inside it.

Dither

The editorial version.

For posters, release covers and articles, the map goes one-bit: Signal pixels on Tunnel, an 8 × 8 ordered dither, light falling from the active server. It reads like an old engraving made of packets.

Pattern
Bayer 8 × 8, square pixels, no smoothing
Pixel
4–8px on screen, 0.5mm in print
Colors
Signal on Tunnel, or Tunnel on Paper

Chapter 08

Motion

08

One long move in the whole product: when a connection starts, a pane of glass slides over the map. Everything else is short and quiet.

Do

  • Slide the pane left to right on connect, right to left on disconnect.
  • Change colors with the standard curve in 200ms.
  • With reduced motion, crossfade in 120ms instead.

Don't

  • Bounce, overshoot or spring.
  • Spin the logo or animate it while loading.
  • Move the map itself. The world stays still; the glass moves.
